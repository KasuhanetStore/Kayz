Website for Small Business in Malaysia: 7 Things to Check Before You Order

A useful business website should do more than look attractive. It should explain what you offer, answer basic questions and make the next step obvious for a potential customer.

1. Decide what the website must achieve

Start with one clear goal: receive enquiries, introduce a service, display products, publish information or support online orders. A focused goal helps prevent unnecessary features and keeps the project within budget.

2. Choose a page structure that fits the business

A single landing page can work for a focused offer or campaign. A business with several services, locations, product categories or regular updates may need a multi-page website and a content management system (CMS). Do not pay for a complex structure if the business will not use it.

3. Make mobile usability a requirement

Many visitors will open a link from a phone. Check that text is readable, buttons are easy to tap, images fit the screen and contact options are visible without excessive scrolling.

4. Give visitors a clear way to contact you

Use a clear WhatsApp button, enquiry form, phone number or email address. Ask only for information needed to respond. A short, working enquiry path is usually more useful than a long form that visitors abandon.

5. Understand the published package scope

The Premium Standard package starts at RM900++ and is intended for a multi-page website with a custom CMS, product or activity gallery, enquiry or booking forms, social links and advanced SEO. The exact scope must be confirmed against the project requirements.

For repairs, upgrades, extra functions or integrations, the Custom Web Workshop assesses the existing website and requirements separately. Domain, hosting, third-party and payment-provider costs should only be treated as included when the written quotation says so.

6. Ask about content, access and ownership

Before work begins, agree who supplies text, images, logos and product details. Confirm who controls the domain, hosting and key accounts. Never send passwords or one-time codes through a normal enquiry form or chat.

7. Check the launch essentials

Before launch, test the website on mobile and desktop, check every button and form, review page titles and descriptions, confirm HTTPS, and make sure the sitemap and important pages can be crawled. A launch checklist catches basic issues before customers do.

No. A custom domain is not included in the published Landing Page package.

Does every business need a CMS?

No. A CMS is useful when the business needs to update pages or content regularly. A simple landing page may not need one.

These functions require a separate scope check. Availability, integration effort and third-party fees depend on the existing system and the selected providers.
